# Idea Pool C — random-seeded brainstorm

Pool for OpusLegends (single-level 2D canvas rhythm-platformer, assumed ~150 BPM riff-heavy track).
Timing reference at 150 BPM: **beat = 400 ms, bar = 1.6 s, 8th = 200 ms, 16th = 100 ms, 8-bar phrase = 12.8 s.**

Ideas were seeded from outside sources on purpose so they don't drift toward "knight + lava + goblin".
Every idea has a `Src:` tag pointing at the source list below, and a score:
**O** = originality, **F** = fun potential, **C** = feasibility in 2D procedural canvas (each 1-5).
~~Struck-through~~ ideas are rejected, with a one-line reason.

## 1. HERO concepts

**H1. Nell the Knocker-Up** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: u-knock, s-rest
A lanky night-owl girl in a long coat and flat cap carrying a bamboo pole three times her height. At small size she reads as a stick figure with one long diagonal line, which is very distinctive. Personality: wide awake while the world sleeps, and cheerfully rude about it. Movement: she pole-vaults (jump plants the pole, and a second tap on the next beat vaults higher). Attack: *Rap-rap-rap* is a triplet of pole taps that can hit three targets in a vertical column. A pea-shooter handles hi-hat pickups.
Signature beat move: **Triple Rap** on the three hits of a snare fill.

**H2. Gille** — O4 F5 C4 · Src: u-binche
A Binche carnival dancer: an enormous white ostrich-plume hat (half the sprite's height), a pale wax mask, a stuffed hump, bells and wooden clogs. The plume bobs and splays on every beat, so the beat is visible on the hero himself. Movement: a heavy, stompy run with bells jingling on 8ths. Attack: **Clog Stomp** is a ground-pound that sends a shockwave along the floor. **Orange Lob** throws a fruit in an arc that lands exactly one beat later.
Signature beat move: stomp on the kick and lob on the snare. The hero's two attacks are the drum kit.

**H3. Vorti** — O5 F5 C5 · Src: u-vort, s-helix
A translucent, glowing bell-shaped microbe perched on a coiled helix spring-stalk, with a crown of flickering cilia. Drawing it takes a gradient bell, a sine-wave coil and radial lines. It doesn't run: it *boings*. Holding the button compresses the spring (the coil visibly tightens with each 8th), and releasing it snaps Vorti forward or up. Personality: twitchy and hyper, and it flinches at loud noises (a camera jolt on crashes).
Signature beat move: **Snap-Coil**. Hold through the snare roll and release on the downbeat for a huge launch. Hold-and-release timing is a different skill from tap-on-beat.

**H4. The Wooden Spoon** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: w-wig
The worst-scoring maths graduate at Cambridge: a scruffy kid in a flapping black gown and a mortarboard, dragging a giant wooden spoon. Underdog energy. Attack: **Scoop-and-Fling** takes two beats. On beat 1 the spoon scoops an enemy or object, and on beat 2 it flings it forward along a parabola into a target. The spoon also works as a pogo.
Signature beat move: every scoop is answered by a fling, so the hero's attack is itself call-and-response.

**H5. River Pig** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: w-mont (log driving)
A stocky log driver in a red-checked wool shirt and spiked boots, holding a peavey (a pole with a swinging hook). He never touches dry land; he runs on logs. Attack: **Peavey Hook**, which hooks a log end, flips it upright and vaults off it. Personality: loud and fearless, and he sings along with the riff.
Signature beat move: hook on the beat, and the flipped log lands on the next one.

**H6. Ttakun & Herrena (txalaparta twins)** — O5 F3 C3 · Src: w-rahm (txalaparta)
Two Basque siblings carrying a long plank between them. The player controls the front twin, and the back twin copies each input one 8th later, which produces the interlocking txalaparta rhythm by itself. The silhouette (two people plus a plank) is unique.
Signature beat move: **Plank Slam**. Both twins slam the plank down for a double hit, a flam.
*Risk:* the echo twin could make hitboxes confusing. Needs prototyping.

**H7. Heqa the Offended** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: met-5, s-off
A tiny, furious Egyptian scribe in a white kilt, with a reed pen behind his ear and a scroll under his arm. He is permanently outraged. Attack: **Strongly Worded Letter** throws a papyrus scroll that unrolls along the ground on the riff, flattening enemies in a line of hieroglyphs. Pickups are grain rations, and he counts them out loud ("four heqat!").
Signature beat move: the scroll unrolls one glyph per 8th across a two-beat riff phrase.

**H8. Gurgle** — O5 F5 C5 · Src: w-kor
A giant Gippsland earthworm with a purple head and a blue-grey segmented body. It swims through the ground in sine arcs and breaches the surface on beats. Very cheap to draw: a chain of circles following a path. **Every on-beat hit adds a segment**, so your combo is visibly the worm's length. The worm gurgles (bass wobble) when it dives.
Signature beat move: **Breach**. Surface on the downbeat, eat the note in mid-air, and dive on the next beat.

**H9. Pyro** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: u-pyro
A walking pyrophone: a body of glass organ-pipes with little flames dancing inside, and stubby brass feet. Every jump fires a flame burst out of the pipes, and each pipe is a note. Personality: excitable and slightly dangerous.
Signature beat move: **Detonation Hop**. The jump is an explosion, so it must be pressed on the kick to "fire". Firing off-beat gives a smaller, sputtering hop.

**H10. Stormcock** — O4 F3 C4 · Src: w-wig (Blackwall tugs)
A stubby Thames tugboat with a rooster's comb and wattles on its funnel, waddling on duck feet. It crows the vocal hook. Attack: **Tow Line**, a rope harpoon that hooks enemies or anchors and yanks on the beat.
Signature beat move: crow-and-yank. It's charming, but it's a boat on land.

~~**H11. Hedgehog**~~ — O2 F3 C5 · Src: w-cro (Hedgehog mortar)
A spiky ball that fires 24 spines in an ellipse. *Rejected: it reads instantly as Sonic, and the spines idea works better as an obstacle (see M10).*

~~**H12. The Owl Conductor**~~ — O3 F2 C4 · Src: met-1
*Rejected as a hero: an owl flaps and glides, which fights a sprint-right platformer. Moved to W4 as the world's conductor.*

## 2. WORLD / THEME concepts

**W1. Sugar Loaf Falls** — O4 F5 C4 · Src: w-mont
An 83 m waterfall at night. The water glows iron-yellow, and a giant white "sugar loaf" ice cone sits at its base. Palette: deep indigo sky, molten gold water, ice cyan, and pink firework bursts. Parallax (back to front): stars and fireworks; cliffs of the Canadian Shield; the falls as a vertical ribbon of animated gradient stripes; the suspension bridge; the ice cone and foreground ice. **How it performs:** the bridge cables are strings that pluck when you land on them, fireworks burst on the crash cymbals, and the falls' stripes scroll at the riff's 16th rate.

**W2. The Spring Drive** — O4 F5 C4 · Src: w-mont (log driving)
A roaring spring-melt river crammed with logs. The wanigan kitchen-raft chugs alongside with smoke from its stovepipe. Palette: peat-brown water, white foam, birch white, and red-checked shirts. Parallax: pine silhouettes; far logs; near logs as platforms; foam sprays. **How it performs:** logs bob on the kick, spin on the riff and pile into a jam during build-ups. The level's structure is "the jam builds, then it blows".

**W3. The Stalacpipe Cavern** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: u-stal
A vast cave where tuned stalactites hang like organ pipes, with mallets on armatures tapping them. Palette: near-black, amber lantern light, and mineral teal and rose on the formations. Parallax: deep darkness with glints; distant stalactite forests; near columns. **How it performs:** the cave *is* the lead instrument. Every melodic note of the riff is a visible mallet strike somewhere on screen, with a ripple ring and a dust puff.

**W4. The Concert of Birds (living embroidery)** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: met-1
The whole level is an 18th-century embroidered panel. Everything is drawn with stitch-dash strokes on a procedurally crosshatched linen background, in faded silk greens, peacock blue and oxblood. A huge owl sits in the central tree conducting, and magpies, parrots and a peacock line the branches. Parallax: the linen weave; background stitched trees; branch platforms in the foreground. **How it performs:** each bird species is an instrument layer. Birds you pass "wake up" and join in, so the song literally gains layers as you progress. At the climax the embroidery unravels into threads.

**W5. Binche at Dawn** — O5 F5 C4 · Src: u-binche
Cobbled carnival streets at sunrise, windows covered in wire netting against flying oranges. Hundreds of Gilles stomp in the background as a rhythmic silhouette wall, their plumes rising and falling in unison. Palette: dawn peach, blood orange, white plumes, and the black, yellow and red of the costumes. **How it performs:** the crowd is the drum kit. The back row stomps on the kick, the middle row shakes bells on the hats, and oranges arc across on the snare.

**W6. The Tape Loop Mill** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: w-cask, met-6
A cavernous industrial hall with hundreds of tape loops strung between mic stands and pulleys, spinning reels as big as Ferris wheels, and VU meters glowing like windows. Palette: oxide brown, pewter chrome, VU green and warning red. **How it performs:** the tape loops are the tracks of the song, and you run *on* the tape. Over the level the tape visibly decays: colour flakes off and gaps appear, a nod to Basinski. A late section speeds the loops up until rhythm becomes pitch (see S6).

**W7. The Galloping Carriage** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: w-rail
You run on the roof of an endless Victorian train. Storybook-watercolour snapshots whip past the side: bridges, houses, hedges, ditches, cattle, a child picking brambles, a tramp staring. Palette: soft greens, cream and steam grey. Parallax: far hills that barely move; near hedges as blurred streaks. **How it performs:** the scenery lands on the beat. Every bridge, post and tunnel mouth passes on a kick or snare, so the landscape itself is the metronome.

**W8. Worm Country** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: w-kor
A cross-section view. Above: velvety rolling dairy hills and a little mining town. Below: coal seams, old mine shafts, and the burrows of giant earthworms. Palette: hill emerald, coal black, worm purple and lamp yellow. **How it performs:** the ground bulges and gurgles with the bassline (sine-deformed terrain). Things tunnelling underground show up as bumps travelling at the tempo.

**W9. The Cider House (Sagardotegi)** — O4 F3 C4 · Src: w-rahm, s-ineb
A Basque cider house: huge chestnut barrels, cider arcing in long streams from barrel taps, and txalaparta players at the far wall. Palette: oak brown, apple green, amber cider. **How it performs:** the floor is txalaparta planks that clack when struck, the cider streams spurt on the snare, and the world sways a little on the swing sections.

**W10. The Great Chain** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: w-cal (Hudson Chain)
A misty river gorge blocked by a colossal iron chain floating on log rafts, with redcoat warships looming in the fog. Palette: slate blue, rust orange, moss. **How it performs:** the chain is a giant struck bell and a travelling wave. Links clank on the beats, and ships ramming it make the drop.

~~**W11. Hunter-Killer Atlantic**~~ — O3 F3 C4 · Src: w-cro
A night-time escort-carrier deck with sonar pings and depth-charge geysers. *Rejected as the main world: the WWII combat tone and grey palette fight the upbeat, whimsical feel. Its sonar ping and Hedgehog mechanics survive as M10 and M11.*

## 3. BEAT-SYNCED MECHANICS / OBSTACLES

**M1. Birling Logs** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: w-mont
*Player:* rides a rolling log and must tap on each kick to keep balance (a counter-step). A missed tap makes you wobble, and two misses dunk you. *Music:* kick = step; the log's spin speed follows the riff's intensity. *Why fun:* it turns "survive" into a rhythm game you do with your feet while jumping between logs. *Telegraph:* a bark ring on the log flashes one beat before each step, and your boots glow on the grid.

**M2. Snap-Coil Launch** — O5 F5 C5 · Src: u-vort, s-helix
*Player:* holds the button to compress a spring (a pad, or Vorti himself). The coil tightens one notch per 8th, up to 4 notches (two beats). Releasing on the downbeat gives a max launch, and an early release gives a weak hop. *Music:* the hold covers a snare roll or build (800 ms) and the release lands on the kick of the next bar. *Why fun:* hold-and-release timing feels completely different from tapping, and a perfect release is a big payoff. *Telegraph:* coil notches light up like a loading bar synced to 8ths, with an audible ratchet click.

**M3. Wake the Windows** — O5 F5 C4 · Src: u-knock, s-rest
*Player:* runs past a terrace of dark windows and raps each one (punch or pole) on its assigned beat. *Music:* each woken sleeper leans out in a nightcap and **sings one syllable of the vocal hook**. Wake them all and the hook is complete; miss one and the line has a hole. *Why fun:* your accuracy is audible as the lyric, and the street lights up behind you. *Telegraph:* windows flicker faintly one beat before their slot, with a "zzz" puff on the beat.

**M4. Txalaparta Answer** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: w-rahm
*Player:* a pair of NPC players ahead bang a 1-bar pattern on planks (the *ttakun*). On the next bar you must answer by stomping the floor planks in the **counter pattern** (the *herrena*): the gaps they left. *Music:* a percussion call-and-response in the breakdown; your answers are the missing hits. *Why fun:* it asks you to hear the negative space, which is fresher than Simon-says repetition. *Telegraph:* the NPCs' strikes glow white, and your planks glow orange where the gaps were.

**M5. Orange Crossfire** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: u-binche
*Player:* Gilles lob oranges from both sides of the street, and you punch each one at its apex. Punched oranges fly into the netted windows as collectibles, while missed ones splat. *Music:* launches come on the kick, and every apex lands exactly on the snare two beats later. The splats are the snare sample. *Why fun:* it's rhythmic juggling with fruit, and the arcs are generous and readable. *Telegraph:* a dotted arc trail with an apex marker that fills in as the orange climbs.

**M6. Phasing Tapes** — O5 F5 C4 · Src: w-cask (Reich phasing)
*Player:* two stacked tape-loop tracks scroll at different rhythms. Track A has a gap every 3 beats and track B every 4 beats, so they only line up **every 12 beats** (3:4 polyrhythm). You hop between tracks and can only drop through to the next section at the moment they align. *Music:* the backing splits into two riffs in 3 against 4, and they re-converge into unison exactly when the gap opens. *Why fun:* it teaches the player a polyrhythm with their body, and the "click" of re-convergence is a real aha moment. *Telegraph:* a coloured stripe on each track; the stripes march toward each other and flash when aligned.

**M7. Ice Steps of the Sugar Loaf** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: w-mont
*Player:* spray from the falls freezes into ice steps on the hi-hat, one step per 8th, and they melt one beat after they form. You climb a staircase that the music is building a moment ahead of you. *Music:* hi-hat = step appears; snare = steps shatter. *Why fun:* a constant pressure to keep up without being a death-wall; the stair is the song's hi-hat drawn in ice. *Telegraph:* frost sparkles on the spot where the next step will form.

**M8. Stalactite Melody** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: u-stal
*Player:* a row of stalactites hangs overhead, and bonking them from below (jump-headbutt or uppercut) plays pitched notes. The first bar plays the riff by itself; on the second bar you replay it by bonking the right stalactites in order and on time. *Music:* the riff's melody. *Why fun:* you *play the song's hook* by platforming. *Telegraph:* each stalactite glows when its note is due on the demo bar; on your bar it gets a faint rim-light one beat ahead.

**M9. Surf the Chain** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: w-cal (Great Chain)
*Player:* runs along the floating chain boom as a whip-wave travels down it (a sine wave travelling at 1 wavelength per bar). Jumping at the wave's crest on the snare gives a huge boost, and jumping in a trough gives a dud. *Music:* crest = snare, trough = kick; the clanking links are 8ths. *Why fun:* it's surfing timing, easy to feel. *Telegraph:* the wave is visible physically, and a spark runs along the crest.

**M10. Hedgehog Volley** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: w-cro (Hedgehog mortar)
*Player:* a mortar on a distant ship throws 24 spiky contact bombs that land ahead in an ellipse, splashing one per 16th across a 1.5-bar drum fill. You weave through the gaps. The bombs are **contact-fused**: a bomb you jump onto *on the beat* explodes under you as a launch pad, and one touched off-beat hurts. *Music:* the drum fill *is* the volley, one bomb per 16th. *Why fun:* turns a hazard into a tool for skilled players. *Telegraph:* landing-zone circles appear as the volley is fired, a bar before impact.

**M11. Sonar Dark** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: w-cro (Huff-Duff, sonar)
*Player:* in a breakdown the screen goes dark. A sonar ping sweeps out from the hero every 2 beats and briefly outlines the platforms, and you jump from memory between pings. *Music:* the ping is a pitched synth hit on beats 1 and 3, and the platforms are timed so jumps land on 2 and 4. *Why fun:* a tense, quiet contrast section that trusts the player's rhythm sense. *Telegraph:* the ping ring itself; platforms keep a faint afterglow that fades over one beat.

**M12. Freeze on the Rest ("Restwards")** — O5 F5 C5 · Src: s-rest, s-off, met-4
*Player:* the song has stop-time moments where the band cuts out for a beat or two (Black Betty-style stabs). During a rest you **must not act**: the Offended (E1) spin round to glare, and anyone caught moving is huffed at and knocked back. You act on the stabs and freeze in the gaps. *Music:* silence is the obstacle. *Why fun:* nobody expects a rhythm platformer to reward *stillness*; it's red light, green light at 150 BPM. *Telegraph:* the Offended inhale (they puff up) one beat before a rest, and a big "shh!" icon appears.

**M13. Mindful Breath (half-time float)** — O4 F3 C5 · Src: s-mind
*Player:* in a half-time section, you hold for 4 beats to inhale (the hero inflates) and release over 4 beats to exhale and float across a big gap. You must match the pad swell; holding too long pops you. *Music:* a swelling pad or vocal "ahh" in a half-time bridge. *Why fun:* a breather after the fast sections. *Telegraph:* a breathing circle HUD pulses with the pad. *Note: possibly too slow for a 150 BPM showcase; keep it short (4 bars max).*

**M14. Double-Helix Stair** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: s-helix, u-helix
*Player:* a rotating DNA-like double helix. Two strands are drawn with sine offsets and depth shading, and they cross each other **once per beat**. You hop from strand to strand at the crossings, and a strand that swings to the "back" becomes intangible. *Music:* crossings = beats; rotation speed doubles in the chorus (crossings on 8ths). *Why fun:* pseudo-3D spectacle from pure 2D sine math, with a clear rule. *Telegraph:* a spark where the strands are about to cross; back strands are dimmed.

**M15. Galloping Meter Switch** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: w-rail
*Player:* on the train roof, obstacles come in straight 8ths, and then the meter switches to a **triplet gallop** (DUM-da-da) for 8 bars. Duck under bridges on DUM and hop over hedges and ditches on the "da-da". *Music:* the song shifts to a shuffle or 12/8 feel. *Why fun:* a rhythm *change* is the challenge, not just density, and it feels like riding a horse or train. *Telegraph:* the train's wheel-clack audibly switches pattern one bar early, and the scenery spacing changes visibly.

**M16. Ride the Worm** — O4 F5 C5 · Src: w-kor
*Player:* mounts the giant earthworm. It breaches in sine arcs every 2 beats, and you steer arc height up or down while it's underground to collect notes along the arc and avoid the mine props. *Music:* dive = bass gurgle wobble; breach = kick plus crash. *Why fun:* it's a mount section with huge motion, and the body trailing behind looks amazing. *Telegraph:* a dirt bulge travels toward the next breach point.

**M17. Spoon Catapults** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: w-wig
*Player:* giant wooden spoons lie on the ground as see-saws. Land on the handle on the kick, and the object in the bowl (a crate, an enemy, or a sleeping Offended) flies up in a parabola and lands on the snare, hitting a gong, a window or a boss. *Music:* the two-beat cause and effect is kick then snare. *Why fun:* one input creates a delayed, satisfying payoff, and chaining spoons makes a Rube Goldberg groove. *Telegraph:* a dotted arc preview from each bowl, with the target lit.

**M18. Bo-taoshi Topple** — O5 F4 C3 · Src: u-bo
*Player:* a tall pole defended by a scrum, with a smug enemy perched on top. You have **exactly 2 bars**: springboard off the attackers' backs, one per beat (they crouch on the beat), to climb, then punch the pole on the four chorus hits to tilt it past 30 degrees. It topples and becomes a bridge. *Music:* the climb is the verse's last 4 beats and the punches are the chorus stabs. *Why fun:* a crowd-chaos mini-set-piece with a hard, readable goal. *Telegraph:* an angle gauge on the pole with a 30-degree mark; backs flash on the beat.

**M19. Pyrophone Gauntlet** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: u-pyro
*Player:* a floor of glass pipes, one per note of the riff. On the demo bar, pipes flare to show the riff, and on the repeat bar you run across and jump only over the pipes that fire. Low notes flare short and high notes flare tall, so **you read the melody's contour as flame height**. *Music:* the riff. *Why fun:* hearing the riff tells you how high to jump. *Telegraph:* the demo bar plus a flicker of pilot light one beat ahead.

**M20. Wax Echo** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: u-binche (wax masks), w-cask (tape delay)
*Player:* a wax-masked double replays your inputs from **one bar ago**, on a parallel lane. Some doors and switches need you and your echo to hit simultaneously, so you plan a bar ahead: "what I do now, the echo does next bar." *Music:* a delay-throw effect on the lead; the echo's actions are heard as quieter delayed hits. *Why fun:* a head-scratcher that stays rhythmic, since you're effectively composing a canon. *Telegraph:* a ghost trail showing where the echo will be; paired switches are colour-matched.

**M21. Tape Scrub Rewind** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: w-cask
*Player:* at DJ-scratch moments the tape scrubs backward, rewinding the world one beat. It's an optional second chance: a hit you missed comes back around once. *Music:* the song's scratch fills. *Why fun:* forgiveness disguised as a musical effect. *Telegraph:* a tape-hiss plus a backwards-spinning reel icon. *Risk: this messes with the audio-clock conductor; the music must actually reverse or skip.*

**M22. Log Jam Build** — O4 F5 C4 · Src: w-mont
*Player:* during an 8-bar build, logs pile into a jam one layer per bar, and the water visibly rises. You climb the growing jam (it is the level) to reach a dynamite plunger at the top, and push it **on the final beat of the build**. *Music:* the build-up; the drop is the explosion. *Why fun:* the player *causes* the drop. *Telegraph:* the plunger glows and a countdown of fuse sparks appears for the last 4 beats. (It escalates into S1.)

~~**M23. Inebriant Sway**~~ — O3 F2 C5 · Src: s-ineb
Gravity tilts left and right on the swing beat, platforms slide, and the camera rolls. *Rejected: nausea risk, and it makes timing feel like the game's fault. Keep only a subtle visual sway in W9.*

~~**M24. Glass-Rod Conveyor**~~ — O4 F2 C4 · Src: u-swan
The floor is rotating twisted glass rods (Archimedes screws) that push you forward or back, reversing each bar. *Rejected: direction flips at 150 BPM are unreadable and feel unfair. Keep the rotating glass rods as a gorgeous water VFX.*

## 4. ENEMY / CREATURE concepts

**E1. The Offended** — O5 F5 C5 · Src: s-off, met-4 (Rowlandson)
Round, red-faced gentlemen drawn like Rowlandson caricatures: huge bellies, tiny wigs, quivering jowls. They bob smugly to the beat. They puff up one beat before a rest and glare during it (M12). Punched on the beat, they deflate with a pitched *harrumph* that plays a bass note, so a row of them is a bassline.

**E2. Magpie Mob** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: met-1
A flock from the embroidery. The owl hoots on the kick, and the magpies answer by diving in a V formation on the snare. Duck, or punch the lead bird to scatter the flock, which gives a flurry of feathers and a 16th-note chatter sample.

**E3. Death-Cap Choir** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: w-kor (Amanita phalloides)
Pale green-capped mushrooms that puff poisonous spore clouds on the 16th-note hi-hats. Their caps snap shut on the downbeat, and that is the only safe moment to bounce on them.

**E4. Gille Stompers** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: u-binche
Masked clog-dancers who stomp on the kick, sending shockwaves along the ground; you jump them. Bop a plume on the beat and the Gille spins around and hands you an orange (a projectile power-up).

**E5. Leaping Brookies** — O3 F4 C5 · Src: w-mont (brook trout)
Speckled trout that leap from the river in arcs on the riff's accents. Their arcs follow the riff's rhythm exactly, so you can hear when they'll jump. You can stand on one at the apex as a stepping stone.

**E6. Vorticella Colony** — O5 F4 C5 · Src: u-vort
Glowing bells on spring stalks rooted in the ground. They stretch up on the kick and snap back on the snare in milliseconds. You pass while they're retracted, or ride one up as it extends.

**E7. The Ration Clerks** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: met-5
Tiny scribes with abacus beads and reed pens. They waddle toward you and confiscate collectibles ("four heqat!"). Punch them on the beat to get your stuff back with interest. *Bonus:* they do the song's **count-in** ("one heqat, two heqat, three heqat, four!").

**E8. Gamecock, Stormcock & Woodcock** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: w-wig (Blackwall tugs)
A trio of rooster-headed tugboats that crow the vocal hook in three-part harmony and puff smoke rings from their funnels on the beats. The smoke rings are platforms for a bar. It's a mini-boss where you knock each tug's comb flat on its line of the hook.

**E9. Pyro Salamanders** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: u-pyro
Lizards with glass-tube bodies and flames inside. The flame height follows the riff's pitch (low note, low flame), so to dodge them you read the melody.

**E10. Sleepwalkers** — O4 F3 C5 · Src: u-knock, s-rest
Lumps in nightcaps shuffling toward you in their sleep, harmless but blocking the way. Rap them on the beat and they wake, startled, then **join the band** by adding a percussion layer (the crowd claps). It converts enemies into music.

**E11. Wax Mimics** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: u-binche, w-cask
Wax-masked doppelgangers that copy your last bar of inputs one bar later (see M20). As enemies, they follow your exact path, so you must "compose" a safe path for them to walk into a hazard.

~~**E12. Human Pyramid**~~ — O3 F3 C2 · Src: u-bo
A pyramid of dozens of small characters that builds up on the build-up and collapses on the drop. *Rejected: dozens of articulated figures is expensive to animate procedurally for a small payoff; M18 captures the Bo-taoshi energy better.*

## 5. SET-PIECE / CLIMAX moments

**S1. Blow the Jam** — O4 F5 C4 · Src: w-mont
After the M22 build, you slam the plunger on beat 4. The music drops out for **one beat of silence**, then the DROP: the whole log jam erupts skyward in slow motion. You run across the tumbling logs mid-air as they spin, each log landing exactly on a chorus beat. The water behind bursts through as a white wall of foam that chases you.

**S2. Thaw of the Sugar Loaf** — O4 F5 C4 · Src: w-mont
You descend the frozen falls. On the drop the ice cracks line by line in time with the snare, and the 83 m waterfall unfreezes and roars back to life. You ride the torrent down on a slab of ice while the fireworks competition goes off behind, with a burst on every crash cymbal and the gold water lit up.

**S3. The Tapestry Unravels** — O5 F5 C3 · Src: met-1
The owl conductor raises its wings for the final chorus. Every bird you woke (W4) sings at once, then the embroidery begins to unravel: threads peel off the linen and whip across the screen. You swing from thread to thread on each beat (a rope-swing chain) as the world un-stitches around you, leaving bare linen. Finally the owl pulls one thread and the hero pops out onto the "back side" of the tapestry (a mirrored, knotted version).

**S4. Snap the Great Chain** — O4 F5 C4 · Src: w-cal
A warship rams the chain boom. The chain snaps link by link **behind you on each beat**, and you sprint along the collapsing chain, the rafts upending, to leap onto the ship's bowsprit on the final crash.

**S5. Orange Storm over Binche** — O5 F5 C4 · Src: u-binche
A thousand Gilles fill the square and throw at once. The sky fills with oranges whose apexes form **the next 4 bars of the beat grid, visible in the air**. You crowd-surf across the bobbing ostrich plumes, punching oranges through windows while the whole square stomps in unison.

**S6. Rhythm Becomes Pitch** — O5 F5 C4 · Src: w-cask (tape loop speed-up)
The final section in the Tape Mill. The loops accelerate and the song's drum pattern speeds up (tempo ramp or pitch-up effect). Obstacles come faster and faster until they merge into a single continuous streak, a wall of sound. At that moment the streak becomes **a solid grind rail**, the rhythm has turned into a tone, and you grind along it on the final held chord. On the last beat the tape snaps: silence, a reel spins loose, and a whip-crack ends the level.

**S7. Worm Breach over Korumburra** — O4 F5 C5 · Src: w-kor
The giant earthworm (300 segments, 300 m here) erupts from the coal mine and arcs over the whole town. You ride its back through the air, with each body segment lighting up as the arc passes the beat. Its gurgle is the drop's sub-bass.

**S8. The Great Stalacpipe Finale** — O4 F4 C4 · Src: u-stal
The cavern's mallets fire in sequence, playing the full riff by themselves, and you race along the stalactite tips as they ring. On the final chord every stalactite drops like a piano key, one per 16th, and you run across the falling keys.

~~**S9. Carrier Night Launch**~~ — O3 F4 C3 · Src: w-cro
A catapult launch off an escort carrier at night, with depth-charge geysers. *Rejected with W11: the war tone clashes, and a plane section breaks the running-hero fantasy.*

## 6. WILD CARDS

**X1. Restwards Reprise (reverse-tape section)** — O5 F5 C5 · Src: s-rest, w-cask
Near the end the tape reverses. The music plays **backwards** (a reversed stem, which sounds amazing) and the hero turns and runs *left* back through a stretch they already played, with obstacles now coming from the other side. This reuses level geometry, so it's cheap content, and it's a "wait, what?!" moment. The song flips forwards again for the final chorus.

**X2. Performance Sampler** — O5 F4 C4 · Src: met-1
Your run is stitched as embroidery thread across the level: perfect beats become neat satin stitches and misses become knots and tangles. On the results screen you get your own **embroidered sampler** of the run, a shareable image that differs for every player.

**X3. The Waking City progress bar** — O4 F4 C5 · Src: u-knock
The level is set at 4 a.m. in a sleeping town. Every on-beat action lights a window somewhere in the parallax background, so by the final chorus the whole skyline is lit, and a sloppy run leaves it half-dark. Progress and score shown *in the world*, with no HUD.

**X4. "Short Backswing" animation rule** — O3 F5 C5 · Src: w-rahm (Rahm's short backswing)
A design rule rather than content: every hero attack has **near-zero wind-up and a huge follow-through**. The contact frame lands exactly on the input or beat, and all the juice (smear, squash, particles) goes *after* the hit. This keeps punches reading as dead on the beat at 150 BPM, where there's no time for anticipation frames.

**X5. Heqanakht's Complaint (framing)** — O5 F3 C5 · Src: met-5, s-off
The level is framed as a furious letter being dictated. An intro card shows a papyrus, the scribe voice counts the song in with ration counts, and the results screen is his follow-up letter grading you ("you received 4 heqat of praise; your brother Snefru did better"). Cheap, memorable personality.

~~**X6. Milspace Ghost Notes**~~ — O3 F3 C2 · Src: w-cal (CALDOL forums)
Players leave forum-style notes where they failed, visible to others. *Rejected: it needs a backend, and it dilutes a tight single-level demo.*
