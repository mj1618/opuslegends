# OpusLegends — Idea Pool B (randomness-seeded)

Every idea below is traced to an outside random source so the pool doesn't drift toward the average
(generic knights, generic lava, generic goblins). Source tags like `[W4]` point to the source list at the bottom.

**Scoring:** `O` = originality, `F` = fun potential, `Fe` = feasibility in 2D canvas (all 1-5).
Rejected ideas are ~~struck through~~ with a one-line reason. Ideas marked ★ are the top 10 (see end).

**Tempo reference (assume 150 BPM, 4/4):** beat = 400 ms, 8th = 200 ms, 16th = 100 ms, bar = 1.6 s,
4-bar phrase = 6.4 s. Anything that telegraphs "one bar ahead" gets 1.6 s of warning, which is plenty.

## 1. HERO concepts (10)

**H1 ★ Bodkin, the Threading Needle** `[S2, S1]` — O5 F5 Fe5
- *Look:* a tall, thin needle-person: a steel-grey sliver with a big oval "eye" hole for a head (the eye of the needle *is* the face: two dots inside the hole). A long ribbon streams behind. The silhouette is a vertical stroke plus a curve of ribbon, so it reads at 24 px.
- *Personality:* cocky and precise, a fencer's swagger; strikes a pose after every hit.
- *Movement:* sprints leaning forward like an arrow; jumps are straight vertical pops.
- *Signature move:* **Thread** is a dash-lunge on the snare. Anything it pierces (enemies, tin stars, rings) gets threaded onto the ribbon like a bead. The ribbon doubles as the combo meter and as the level's main visual flourish.

**H2 ★ Su, the Masked Marshal** `[W4 Sartiglia]` — O5 F4 Fe4
- *Look:* androgynous figure with a blank white porcelain mask, a black top-hat-like headdress wrapped in a veil, a red bolero, and a long lance. Silhouette: tall hat plus a horizontal lance.
- *Personality:* ceremonial and silent, "neither man nor woman, like the land". Never speaks; the crowd cheers for them.
- *Movement:* runs with a galloping stride (a triple-step "da-da-DUM" on each beat, matching a horse gait).
- *Signature move:* **Star Pierce**, a lance thrust that spears hanging tin stars through their centre hole. Secondary move: **The Blessing**, a backflip into a lying-backwards pose that sprays violets across the screen on big downbeats.

**H3 ★ Arroba, the Snail Knight** `[S4, X5 marginalia]` — O4 F5 Fe5
- *Look:* a tiny knight living in a spiral shell shaped exactly like `@`. Running, it's a knight wearing a snail shell as a backpack. Rolling, the whole thing becomes a wheel.
- *Personality:* the medieval-marginalia joke played straight: brave and absurd, with a tiny lance, ready to fight rabbits.
- *Movement:* alternates between running and **rolling**. The spiral on the shell ticks round one quarter-turn per beat (arroba = "one quarter"), so the hero's own body is a visible metronome.
- *Signature move:* **Quarter Roll**. Tap to curl into the shell and roll, and the roll snaps 90° per beat. On the bar's downbeat you uncurl into a lance-punch. Kids will get "roll, roll, roll, POKE" straight away.

**H4 ★ Whose Sleeves? (the Empty Kimono)** `[M1 Tagasode screen, S3 abdat]` — O5 F4 Fe4
- *Look:* a kimono running with no one inside. The only face is two glowing dots in the dark of the collar. Its long hanging sleeves swing wildly, and the pattern on it changes each song section, stamped from a 1667 kosode pattern book.
- *Personality:* playful ghost, mischievous, curious about everything.
- *Movement:* floats slightly while running, so the sleeves trail like flags.
- *Signature moves:* **Sleeve Crack**, a whip-crack with a sleeve on the snare that hits at long range. **Abdat (Hide)**: on the beat, the kimono collapses into a flat pile of cloth on the floor so things pass overhead, then pops back up. Hiding becomes a rhythm verb.

**H5 Puntal, the Prop-Girl** `[S1]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Look:* a stocky stagehand in overalls carrying a pole 3× her height. Silhouette: small body with a huge diagonal line.
- *Personality:* the stagehand who holds the whole show up; unflappable.
- *Movement:* pole-vaults on the downbeat and slides under on the off-beat.
- *Signature move:* **Prop!**, which jams the pole into the ground to hold up falling scenery (see mechanic B4), then kicks it out later so the scenery crushes enemies.

**H6 Crabbe, the Fiddler** `[X2 fiddler crab]` — O4 F5 Fe5
- *Look:* a small crab with one gigantic bright claw (as big as its body). The asymmetric silhouette reads instantly.
- *Personality:* show-off; a courtship dancer who treats the whole level as a date.
- *Movement:* scuttles sideways (so the camera is side-on, and the crab runs "sideways-forward"), and hops.
- *Signature move:* **The Wave**. Raising the big claw on the beat is both the attack and a courtship wave. Every crab NPC nearby joins in sync, so good rhythm builds a growing chorus of claws behind you.

**H7 The Lampshade Diva** `[W3 Barbara Dex Award]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Look:* a performer in a semi-transparent lampshade dress with a glowing bulb inside. Silhouette: trapezoid plus legs plus a halo of light.
- *Personality:* camp, dramatic, Eurovision energy.
- *Movement:* high-heel sprint with twirls.
- *Signature move:* **Spotlight**, a beam cast from the shade on the vocal hook that turns enemies into a cheering audience (they flip to the background layer and applaud on the beat).

**H8 Kabura, the Whistling Archer** `[M2 Yanone, Kabura-ya]` — O4 F4 Fe4
- *Look:* a round-bellied kid whose head is a perforated turnip-bulb, like a kabura-ya arrowhead. It whistles as the kid runs, and the pitch rises with speed.
- *Personality:* noisy and fearless; the whistle is meant to "ward off evil".
- *Signature move:* **Volley**. Arrows fired on the hi-hat, with the count escalating per phrase (see set-piece SP1).

**H9 La Cholita Tromba** `[W7 El Alto / cholets]` — O4 F5 Fe4
- *Look:* bowler hat, two long braids, layered pollera skirts in hot pink and lime. Silhouette: tiny hat on a big bell of skirt. Wrestling energy (the cholitas luchadoras of El Alto; this link came from the El Alto chain plus prior knowledge).
- *Movement:* skirts bell out on every beat as she runs.
- *Signature move:* **Pollera Spin**, a top-spin on the beat where the skirt layers become a saw-blade of colour, and **Bowler Boomerang**, a hat throw that returns on the bar downbeat.
- *Note:* the design must be respectful and celebratory, and it pairs naturally with the cholet world (W1).

## 2. WORLD / THEME concepts (10)

**W1 ★ Cholet Skyline (El Alto at 4,000 m)** `[W7 Mabel Monje → El Alto → Freddy Mamani's Neo-Andean architecture]` — O5 F5 Fe5
- *Setting:* rooftops and facades of cholets: candy pink, lime green and tangerine buildings with circular windows, sharply angled rooflines and stepped chakana (Andean cross) motifs. Inside are ballrooms lit by 2,000-3,000 coloured bulbs and five to seven huge chandeliers.
- *Palette:* deep altiplano cobalt sky, magenta, lime, tangerine and gold, with snow-white on the far peak.
- *Parallax:* (1) snow peak far away, (2) cable-car lines crossing the sky with gondolas, (3) cholet silhouettes, (4) rooftops you run on, (5) foreground string lights.
- *World as instrument:* the string lights and ballroom bulbs are an LED sequencer. Chase patterns run along them on the hi-hats, whole facades flash on the kick, and the chakana steps light up bottom to top on build-ups. It's all rectangles and circles, which makes it perfect for procedural canvas art.

**W2 ★ Lighthouse of Maracaibo** `[X3 Catatumbo lightning]` — O5 F5 Fe5
- *Setting:* a lake of stilt houses under a storm that never stops (16-40 flashes a minute, "the Lanterns of Saint Anthony").
- *Palette:* near-black navy, with electric white and violet in the flashes and warm amber windows in the stilt houses.
- *Parallax:* (1) cloud bank with inner-cloud lightning, (2) far shoreline, (3) stilt-house village, (4) boardwalks you run on, (5) reeds in the foreground.
- *World as instrument:* lightning is the snare. Between flashes, the world is mostly dark. Each flash freeze-frames everything as a white silhouette, the Rayman-style camera flash. The lake surface ripples on the bass.

**W3 ★ The Bas-de-Page (Manuscript Margins)** `[X5 marginalia]` — O5 F5 Fe4
- *Setting:* the hero runs along the bottom margin of a giant illuminated manuscript. The text column above is the "sky", and drolleries (snail knights, lance-wielding rabbits, cats fighting mice) live in the margins.
- *Palette:* vellum cream, lapis blue, vermilion, burnished gold leaf, iron-gall brown ink.
- *Parallax:* (1) facing page seen blurred, (2) text block with giant illuminated initials, (3) vine-scroll borders (the platforms are the vines), (4) ink ground line.
- *World as instrument:* gold leaf glints on the kick, the vines grow a curl on each beat, and illuminated initials bloom open on the chorus. The text "sings": its words highlight in karaoke order with the vocal.

**W4 Blood Mountain** `[W2 FC Kairat → Khan Tengri]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Setting:* a race up a huge marble pyramid peak with geometric ridges that "glows red at sunset" (its local name means blood mountain).
- *Palette:* the whole level is a gradient over time: ice blue and white marble at the start, then gold, then blood red at the finale. The song's arc is literally a sunset.
- *Parallax:* sky gradient, far ridges, glacier séracs, marble ridge path, prayer-flag-like streamers in the foreground.
- *World as instrument:* the ridgelines in the far layer are drawn as the song's waveform, and they pulse.

**W5 Sartiglia Street** `[W4 Oristano Cathedral → Sartiglia]` — O4 F4 Fe4
- *Setting:* a sand-covered street between Baroque facades toward a cathedral. Tin stars hang on ribbons across the street, drummers and trumpeters play from balconies, and the crowd throws violets.
- *Palette:* sand ochre, Baroque cream and terracotta, deep red banners, violet.
- *World as instrument:* the balcony drummers play the actual drum track (visibly hitting on kick and snare), and trumpeters raise their horns for the riff stabs.

**W6 Dune of the Jinn** `[W6 Qods, Semnan → Dasht-e Kavir → Rig-e Jenn]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Setting:* the "desert of spirits". White salt-crust polygon flats give way to towering dunes, and heat shimmer hides jinn.
- *Palette:* verses at night (indigo, salt white, cold cyan) and choruses at noon (blinding white, orange, deep shadow). This follows the desert's 70 °C day-night swing.
- *World as instrument:* the salt polygons are drum pads (see B13), and the dunes "boom" the bassline (singing sand). Heat shimmer is a sine-wave displacement that pulses with the beat.

**W7 The Waterwheel Hammer Mill** `[X4 trip hammer]` — O4 F5 Fe5
- *Setting:* a riverside forge where a waterwheel turns a line shaft that drives dozens of trip hammers, with sparks, bellows and glowing ingots.
- *Palette:* soot black, ember orange, wet slate blue, bright spark yellow.
- *World as instrument:* the camshaft is the sequencer. Its pegs are the notes, and you can SEE upcoming hammer drops as pegs rotating toward the lever (see B5). It's diegetic sheet music, like a barrel organ.

**W8 Whose Sleeves? Folding Screen** `[M1 Met 42671 Tagasode]` — O5 F4 Fe4
- *Setting:* a world inside a six-panel gold-leaf folding screen: gold clouds, lacquered clothing racks, draped robes, and no people.
- *Palette:* gold leaf, black lacquer, vermilion, indigo, and white patterned robes.
- *World as instrument:* the screen panels fold and unfold like an accordion on the beat (see B20). Robes on the racks sway on the swing of the groove.

**W9 The Tilt-Shift Diorama** `[W3 Eurovision 2011 → tilt-shift postcards]` — O3 F4 Fe4
- *Setting:* the level is a hyper-saturated miniature model town seen from a high angle, with a sharp strip in the middle and blur bands above and below.
- *World as instrument:* toy trains, windmills and tiny crowds all move on the beat like wind-up toys, and a giant model-maker's hand places pieces on the downbeats.
- *Risk:* on its own it's a filter rather than a world, so it works best as a framing device for the finale (SP9).

## 3. BEAT-SYNCED MECHANICS / OBSTACLES (23 written, 4 rejected)

**B1 ★ Tinikling Poles** `[X1 Tinikling]` — O5 F5 Fe5
- *Player:* bamboo pole pairs lie across the path, and bearers slide them tap-tap-CLAP. You hop INTO the gap on the taps and OUT before the clap, like the Philippine dance.
- *Music:* the taps are toms/rim clicks and the CLAP is the snare. For 4/4, use tap-tap-tap-CLAP. In the outro the poles speed up just as the dance does, and a final double set forces 16th-note hops.
- *Why fun:* it's rhythm the player feels in their feet: a clear in/out dance rather than a single jump.
- *Telegraph:* the bearers lift the poles visibly on the preceding beat, and the audio click is diegetic.

**B2 ★ Star Pierce** `[W4 Sartiglia, S1 puntal "to pierce"]` — O5 F5 Fe5
- *Player:* tin stars hang on ribbons across the path, each swinging like a pendulum with a 1-bar period. The centre hole lines up with your lance height only on the beat. Attack then to spear it.
- *Music:* stars on the snare, and big stars on the chorus downbeat ("douze points!").
- *Why fun:* a precision hit with a satisfying *shing*. Speared stars stack on the lance as a visible trophy stick.
- *Telegraph:* pendulum motion is readable physics, and a star glints one beat early.

**B3 ★ The Thread (bead combo)** `[S2 bodkin: ribbon-threading needle]` — O5 F5 Fe4
- *Player:* every on-beat hit threads the target onto your trailing ribbon as a bead. The ribbon is physical: when you dash through a narrow gap (a ring, a keyhole, a star's hole), the whole thread follows. If you mistime, the thread snaps and the beads scatter as collectibles you can re-grab over the next 2 bars.
- *Music:* the threading "zip" is quantised to 8ths, so a long ribbon zipping through a hole plays a bass-riff run.
- *Why fun:* the combo meter is diegetic and beautiful, and losing it is a recoverable drama.
- *Telegraph:* holes glow with the ribbon's colour one bar ahead.

**B4 Prop It Up!** `[S1 puntal]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* on the drop, ceilings and slabs start collapsing. Press PROP on the downbeat to jam a pole and hold them up, then run under. On the next phrase, kick the props out to drop the slabs onto the enemies behind you.
- *Music:* kick = prop, crash cymbal = collapse.
- *Why fun:* you build the level's architecture yourself, then demolish it as a weapon.
- *Telegraph:* dust trickles and cracks spread one bar before a collapse.

**B5 ★ Camshaft Preview** `[X4 trip hammer]` — O5 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* a giant rotating camshaft sits in the background layer. Its pegs are the next 2 bars of hammer drops. Run under the hammers in the gaps and ride a hammer up on its lift to reach high ledges.
- *Music:* hammers are the kick, and double pegs are a double-kick fill.
- *Why fun:* readable foresight. It's sheet music you can see, and a note-highway that makes sense in the world.
- *Telegraph:* the pegs themselves, which are always 2 bars ahead.

**B6 Lightning Memory** `[X3 Catatumbo]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* long stretches are near-dark. Each snare-lightning flash reveals the platforms for 150 ms as a white silhouette, and a faint afterimage outline lingers. You run from memory and rhythm.
- *Music:* the snare, plus bursts of flashes on the fills.
- *Why fun:* tension and release, and trusting the beat. The beat is *literally* what lets you see.
- *Telegraph:* the beat itself, with a low thunder rumble 1 beat before big flashes. Accessibility: add a "dim not dark" option.

**B7 ★ Lead the Wave** `[X2 fiddler crab synchronous waving]` — O5 F5 Fe4
- *Player:* on a beach of fiddler crabs, the crabs wave in a synchronised "stadium wave" whose crests are platforms. Hit your action slightly ON the beat to *lead* the wave (real fiddler crabs gain by leading the synchrony), and the crabs then follow your timing and build crests where you need them.
- *Music:* the wave travels at one crab per 8th note, and the chorus doubles it.
- *Why fun:* you conduct the environment instead of just surviving it.
- *Telegraph:* the crabs' claws pre-raise, and the wave's direction is always visible.

**B8 Arrow Wall Climb** `[M2 Kabura-ya → Kurikara volleys]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* enemy archers fire volleys at the cliff face. The arrows stick in, and those embedded arrows become the pegs you wall-jump up.
- *Music:* each volley is a hi-hat roll, so the peg pattern equals the hi-hat pattern and you climb the hi-hat.
- *Why fun:* the enemy's attack becomes your staircase.
- *Telegraph:* the arrows whistle (kabura-ya), and the pitch tells you the height.

**B9 Whistle Call-and-Response** `[M2 Kabura-ya, M5 Kongo whistle]` — O3 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* whistling arrows fly overhead playing a 1-bar melodic phrase (the vocal hook, or a lead-guitar lick). The next bar, targets pop up and you punch them in the same rhythm.
- *Music:* the vocal hook or guitar lead.
- *Why fun:* a musical conversation.
- *Telegraph:* the phrase itself.
- *Note:* the concept is close to Simon-says, which is why O is only 3.

**B10 ★ Abdat, Hide on the Beat** `[S3 abdat "may he hide", M1 empty robes]` — O5 F5 Fe5
- *Player:* a giant searchlight eye (a moon, a lighthouse, a manuscript's gold-leaf eye of God) sweeps across on the vocal hook. At the exact beat it looks, press HIDE. The hero collapses into a pile, a shell or a shadow, then springs back up. Hiding too early wastes speed, and too late means you're spotted.
- *Music:* the eye opens on the vocal hook's accent, and the "look" beats are the stabs in the riff.
- *Why fun:* a stealth game at 150 BPM. It's a completely different verb from jump or punch, and funny when the eye squints suspiciously at the pile.
- *Telegraph:* the eye's lid visibly lifts one beat ahead, with a drum pickup.

**B11 Recontrast Flip** `[S5]` — O3 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* on each downbeat of the chorus, the palette inverts. Pink platforms exist in the positive image and green ones in the negative, so you plan jumps across the flip.
- *Music:* bar downbeats and the crash.
- *Why fun:* planning across the beat instead of reacting.
- *Telegraph:* the outline of the next palette's platforms is always faintly drawn.
- *Note:* this has been done before (Bit.Trip, Ikaruga-ish colour switches), hence O3.

**B13 ★ Salt-Pad Drumming** `[W6 Dasht-e Kavir salt polygons]` — O4 F5 Fe5
- *Player:* hexagonal salt-crust tiles are drum pads. Landing on-beat plays a drum hit that joins the mix and lights the tile. Off-beat landings crack the tile, which then crumbles 1 bar later.
- *Music:* the kick/tom pattern. You literally play the drum fills in the 4-bar gaps the song leaves open.
- *Why fun:* you're a band member, not a follower.
- *Telegraph:* the "correct" tiles glow faint white on the beat grid, and the fill gap has no backing drums, so the silence invites you in.

**B14 Mirror Runner** `[M3 Utamaro, "Using Two Mirrors to Observe Her Coiffure"]` — O4 F4 Fe4
- *Player:* a horizontal mirror line splits the screen. Your reflection runs upside-down beneath and auto-collects the off-beat notes, but hazards differ above and below. A jump for you is a dive for it.
- *Music:* you take the beats and the reflection takes the "and"s: a built-in groove.
- *Why fun:* two-body rhythm without two sets of controls.
- *Telegraph:* the mirror surface ripples where the reflection's hazards will be.

~~**B15 Pariglia Lane-Hop** `[W4 Sartiglia pariglie acrobatics on 2-3 horses]`~~ — O3 F3 Fe3 — REJECT: depth lanes in a side-on 2D view read poorly and it's close to generic lane runners. Keep the imagery for set-piece SP2.

**B16 Chandelier Fling** `[W7 cholet ballrooms]` — O3 F5 Fe5
- *Player:* chandeliers swing with a 2-beat period. Grab at the apex (on the beat) to get a slingshot fling, and the bulbs along the string lights chase toward the correct release beat.
- *Music:* half-time swing on the bass groove, with releases on the snare.
- *Why fun:* big flowing arcs, glitter particles everywhere, and it's easy to feel.
- *Telegraph:* the string-light chase.

**B17 Light-Wave Relay** `[X6 pyrosome: waves of light travel along the colony]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* long glowing tubes are dark and intangible. Hitting one end sends a wave of light along it, one segment per 8th note, and only lit segments are solid. You race along just behind the wave front.
- *Music:* the wave speed is tied to the 8th-note hi-hat, and branching tubes split on the riff.
- *Why fun:* you are chasing the beat, visibly.
- *Telegraph:* the wave front itself.

**B18 ★ Heartbeat Floor** `[W3 Eurovision 2011: "Feel Your Heart Beat", pink lights pulsing with heartbeats]` — O4 F5 Fe5
- *Player:* at section intros, the floor pulses pink with a lub-dub. Jumping exactly on the "dub" rebounds you into a super-jump twice as high to reach a secret upper route.
- *Music:* the kick's double-hit pattern (lub-dub = kick-kick).
- *Why fun:* an optional expert route and a trampoline you feel in your chest.
- *Telegraph:* the floor visibly bulges on the "lub", which is literally the warning beat.

**B19 Squeeze Bodkin** `[S2 "sit bodkin": wedged between two people]` — O4 F3 Fe5
- *Player:* two big bouncing enemies close in from both sides on the kick. Get wedged between them, then tap the hi-hat 8ths to squirt out upward like a pip, launching high.
- *Music:* kick closes the gap and hi-hat 8ths power the escape.
- *Why fun:* a funny squash-and-stretch moment and a rhythmic "button-mash" that isn't mashing.
- *Telegraph:* the enemies puff up one beat before they slam.

**B20 Folding-Screen Accordion** `[M1 Met 42671 six-panel screen]` — O5 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* terrain is a zigzag folding screen that snaps flat (a bridge) on beats 1 and 3 and zigzags into peaks on 2 and 4. Run the flats, or use the rising peaks as springboards.
- *Music:* the kick/snare alternation. Kick = flat, snare = fold.
- *Why fun:* the ground itself dances, which is very Rayman.
- *Telegraph:* panels crease-shade one beat ahead.

**B21 Dune Surf Sustain** `[W6 Rig-e Jenn / singing dunes]` — O4 F4 Fe5
- *Player:* slide down booming dunes by holding slide through sustained bass notes and releasing on the rests. Held notes are drawn as long glowing sand-ribbons.
- *Music:* the bass sustain, so a HOLD verb instead of a tap.
- *Why fun:* it varies input feel (tap vs hold) and gives the flow state of surfing.
- *Telegraph:* the ribbon length on the dune ahead.

## 4. ENEMIES / creatures that react to the music (11 written, 1 rejected)

**E1 ★ Snail Knights** `[X5 marginalia: knights vs snails]` — O4 F5 Fe5
Armoured snails with a lance and a pennant. They retract into the shell on the KICK (invulnerable, with a *tonk* sound if you hit) and pop out on the SNARE (vulnerable, and they squeal). They teach kick vs snare without a tutorial.

**E2 Rabbit Levy** `[X5 marginalia: rabbits attacking]` — O4 F4 Fe5
Columns of spear-wielding rabbits march in formation on the hi-hat. When the riff changes, the formation re-shapes (a wall, a V, a staircase), so the rabbits' formation is a readable picture of the new riff.

**E3 ★ Torch Oxen** `[M2 → Battle of Kurikara]` — O5 F5 Fe4
Oxen with flaming torches on their horns stampede on the downbeat of each bar. Hop across their backs, since each ox's back is a platform arriving on the beat. Torch flames leave fire-trail ribbons that trace the rhythm.

**E4 ★ Echo Robes** `[M1 Tagasode "whose sleeves?", S3 abdat]` — O5 F5 Fe4
Floating empty kimonos that replay YOUR previous bar of inputs one bar later, like a canon or round. Jump twice and they jump twice next bar. Beat them by baiting them with your own rhythm (for example, feint a jump into a spike row). Eerie, clever and musical.

**E5 Jinn Shimmer** `[W6 Rig-e Jenn]` — O4 F4 Fe4
Invisible heat-distortion figures. They are solid only on the off-beat "and", so you hit them in the gaps between drum hits. This teaches syncopation.

**E6 Lead Crab** `[X2 fiddler crab]` — O4 F4 Fe5
A crowd of rival crabs waves in sync, and one leads (it waves a hair earlier and wears a gold claw). Hit the leader and the chorus falls apart into chaos and drops coins; hit a follower and nothing happens. It's a "find the leader by the rhythm" puzzle.

**E7 Helve Beasts** `[X4 trip hammer]` — O4 F4 Fe5
Long-necked beaked creatures pivoting on a fulcrum like a tilt hammer, nodding on the kick. Pass under during the "up", or jam a prop (Puntal) under the beak to disable them.

**E8 ★ Lampshade Jury** `[W3 Barbara Dex Award, Eurovision scoreboard]` — O5 F4 Fe5
Three judges on stilts hold scorecards. After each of your on-beat moves they flip cards (pink for 8/10/12, purple for 1-7, like the 2011 scoreboard), and the cards become platforms whose height equals the score. Play well and the jury literally builds you a higher road.

**E9 Mirror Maidens** `[M3 Utamaro]` — O4 F3 Fe4
Enemies that exist only in reflections (puddles, mirrors, lacquer). To hit them, attack the place in the real world that corresponds to their reflected position, on the beat. Pairs with B14.

**E10 Pyro-Pickles** `[X6 pyrosome]` — O3 F4 Fe5
Chains of glowing tube creatures that flash when you flash (on your hits). Strike one on the beat and the light cascades down the chain on 16ths, popping each one in sequence like a snare roll.

~~**E11 Copper Golems of Corocoro** `[W7 Mabel Monje → Corocoro copper mines]`~~ — O1 F2 Fe4 — REJECT: a "golem made of ore" is the textbook mean enemy; nothing musical about it.

## 5. SET-PIECES / CLIMAX moments (9 written, 1 rejected)

**SP1 ★ The Kurikara Build-Up** `[M2 → Battle of Kurikara]` — O5 F5 Fe4
Across a ravine, two armies exchange ritual volleys of **15 arrows, then 30, then 50, then 100**, as in the real 1183 battle. Each volley is a longer hi-hat/snare roll, so the level IS the song's build-up. You climb the arrow pegs (B8) as the volleys thicken. On the drop, the torch oxen (E3) stampede down the pass. You leap onto the lead ox and ride it in a river of fire-trails. It's the "tell your friends" moment, and it's historically true.

**SP2 ★ The Blessing Lap** `[W4 Sartiglia: rider lies backwards on galloping horse, blessing the crowd with violets]` — O5 F5 Fe4
In the final chorus the hero leaps onto a galloping horse, lies *backwards* along its back facing the sky, and every beat you press sprays violets over the crowd. The crowd roars, flower bursts bloom full-screen on the crashes, and 2-3 horses gallop in formation with acrobats (the pariglia) alongside. It's a victory lap you play rather than watch.

**SP3 ★ The Storm Chorus** `[X3 Catatumbo]` — O4 F5 Fe5
The stilt-house village in darkness: lightning on every snare. At the drop, the lightning goes continuous and every one of your hits freezes the frame as a white silhouette strobe (the Rayman Castle Rock "camera flash" moment, but it's the sky doing it).

**SP4 ★ The Ballroom Drop** `[W7 cholets]` — O5 F5 Fe5
You sprint up a cholet facade through the build-up, crash through a circular window on the downbeat of the drop, and all 3,000 bulbs and seven chandeliers ignite at once. The ballroom guests (cholitas and dancers) dance in sync with your inputs, and you chandelier-swing (B16) across the ceiling.

**SP5 Sunset Summit** `[W2 → Khan Tengri]` — O4 F4 Fe5
The final climb up the marble ridge: each bar the mountain gets redder. You hit the summit on the song's final beat, the sun drops, and the entire peak glows blood-red with your silhouette on top.

**SP6 ★ The Page Turn** `[X5 marginalia]` — O5 F4 Fe4
In the manuscript world, the song's breakdown turns the page. A giant vellum page sweeps over the screen across 4 bars, and you run up the curling page edge as it lifts, then land on the next spread with a new illustrated scene. (It's a warped-quad animation, which is feasible with canvas transforms plus a gradient shadow.)

**SP7 Screen Fold Finale** `[M1 folding screen]` — O4 F4 Fe4
The whole world folds up like a six-panel screen, panel by panel on each beat of the last bar, and you leap off the last panel as it slams shut on the final hit.

**SP8 The Crab Stadium** `[X2 fiddler crab]` — O4 F5 Fe5
A beach of thousands of fiddler crabs (cheap to draw as tiny sprites). Once you lead the wave (B7), your jumps send waves rippling across the whole beach, which ends up a sea of claws in synchrony with you: spectacle driven by player input.

~~**SP9 Tilt-Shift Reveal: "it was all a model"** `[W3 tilt-shift]`~~ — O2 F3 Fe4 — REJECT: the "pull back to reveal it was a toy" twist is a well-worn ending and it deflates the energy right at the climax.

## 6. WILD CARDS (6 written, 1 rejected)

**X1 ★ The Ballad Fake-Out** `[W1 "Un Hombre Normal", a sentimental ballad]` — O5 F5 Fe5
Two-thirds through, the song stops dead, a cheesy sentimental ballad plays for 4 bars, and every enemy stops attacking and sways with a lighter. The player can walk or dance. Then the riff kicks back in at double intensity. It's a comedic breath, and the drop hits harder afterwards. (It works with any song if we record or produce a 4-bar ballad sting.)

**X2 ★ The Wrong-Clock District** `[W8 Time in Abkhazia: a region running on another country's clock]` — O5 F4 Fe5
A section of the level runs on "a different time zone": everything is offset by one 8th note, so the district's clocks visibly read wrong and all the hazards land on the "and". It's a playful, diegetic way to teach syncopation. The clocks snap back to "our" time when you exit.

**X3 The Band Joins You** `[W3 "Feel Your Heart Beat", B13 salt pads]` — O3 F5 Fe4
Stem-based adaptive mix: the song starts as just drums and bass, and each clean streak brings in the next stem (rhythm guitar, lead, vocals). A miss thins the mix back one layer. It's not new (Rez, Thumper and Crypt of the NecroDancer have relatives), but the feedback is powerful.

**X4 Hidden Words in the Margins** `[X5 marginalia: hidden messages in borders]` — O4 F3 Fe5
The collectibles are illuminated letters, and collecting them in order spells the song's hook lyric. The complete word lights up a final bonus room, which rewards replaying for 100%.

**X5 ★ Costume from the Pattern Book** `[M1 neighbour 42669: 1667 Kosode Pattern Book; M4 85326 1993 silk evening dress]` — O4 F4 Fe5
Each section re-stamps the hero's costume with a new pattern (waves, cranes, chrysanthemums, lightning, chakana), procedurally generated from a small pattern grammar. Streaks unlock rarer patterns, and the end screen shows your "look" for the run. It's cheap cosmetic juice that makes every run feel personal.

~~**X6 Finnies (fish-money)** `[S6 finny = £5 note]`~~ — O3 F2 Fe5 — REJECT: a cute pun, but "schools of banknote-fish as collectibles" adds nothing a normal collectible trail doesn't, and it muddles the visual language.
